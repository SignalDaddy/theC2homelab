## Systems Check
Today marks the first entry of **The C2 Homelab** blog this is basically a test run of my work thus far. It is January 30th, likely the final day of government operations before an extended shutdown, but here in the lab, 
the lights are on and the fans are humming.

Starting this blog for me is a three-fold mission: part thought experiment, part portfolio building, and of course part hobby. While the blog itself is a small feature of my homelab, 
it serves as a live environment for me to master **CI/CD pipelines**. The workflow is lean: content is authored here from my workstation, pushed to GitHub, and automatically deployed to my **AWS S3 bucket**. 

By adopting modern frameworks like **Hugo** and **Blowfish**, I’m forcing myself into a cycle of repetition. Every morning follows a specific protocol: get out of bed, feed the dogs, weigh myself, sit down to code.
I believe that efficiency is a byproduct of consistency; the more I repeat this cycle, the sharper I become at it and by extention all the skills associated with it: DevOps, Cloud Networking, UI/UX, etc...

## Bridging the Skill Gap
I’ve been a soldier and working in Signal and Communications for the majority of my adult life. If you ask me about U.S. Army enterprise architecture or tactical cyber operations, 
I can probably give you the answer confidently and quickly or if I can't I can find the person who has it within minutes, seriously. My military sphere of influence is wide, but 
it is also specialized...

There is where I have a problem, I recognize a looming challenge, **The Civilian Skill Gap.** If you asked me to architect the network for a global bank or a fast-paced SaaS startup, 
my military-centric toolkit would likely fall short. This realization leads to two hard truths that I have to reocognize.

1. **The Skill Gap is Real.** My expertise are as what we call a "green-suiter". The equipment I use needs to be translated into the language of cloud providers and data centers and cyber security firms.
2. **Options Require Growth.** I don't just want a job; I want an outrageous number of options. Whether I end up an SRE at Google, a CISO for a startup, or a Solutions Architect living as an expat, 
I need skills that hold value no matter who I work for, where they are, and what they want me to do.

## The "Over the Hill" Pivot
In the Army, your MOS (Military Occupational Specialty) is your lane. It’s rigid. While the military is starting to recognize the necessity of in-house Software Engineering and DevOps, those roles 
aren't going to be avalilable to those of us "over the hill" meaning, I'm closer to seperating from the Army than I am from joining. I love the Army and I'm sure on some level it loves me too,
but the understandable and inevidable truth is.... the Army is done dumping resources into sharpening my individual skills.

Since I can't always do this work in an official capacity, I will make do in the lab. This blog is the documentation of that transition. 

**Status: Initialized. Mission: Ongoing.**
